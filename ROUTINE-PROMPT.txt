Task: Produce today's 30-day security outlook for "Risk Radar – G7 & EU" and publish it to the public website (GitHub repo afuglesang/riskradar-g7, served at https://afuglesang.github.io/riskradar-g7/). The repository is the only data store: there is no Claude artifact for this system. The site is public and will later move to its own domain, so write for an informed general audience: sober, precise, neutral and analytical, with no marketing language or AI clichés. Never mention Flocean, any company's commercial interests, or the author. Everything you need is in this prompt and in the repository.

1. Load the template. In the cloned repo, read data/manifest.json and take the newest date in "snapshots"; load data/snapshots/<date>.json as today's template. If the manifest is empty (first run), BOOTSTRAP: build the snapshot from data/model.json. For the global part (10 global factors with IDs ai, social, bio, ukraine, mideast, taiwan, nuclear, hybrid, econ, climate, plus global drivers, calendar and watch) you may use the newest public snapshot of the sister site as a starting structure: read https://afuglesang.github.io/riskradar/data/manifest.json, then https://afuglesang.github.io/riskradar/data/snapshots/<newest>.json, and copy only the top-level factors, drivers, method, calendar and watch (not its norway or countries objects). Then verify and update every number yourself; from the second run on, this system is fully independent of the sister site.

Snapshot schema (keep exactly): id and date (today, Europe/Oslo, YYYY-MM-DD), generatedAt (ISO time with +01:00/+02:00), horizonDays 30, horizon ("Rolling 30 days (to <date+30>)"), headline, globalSummary, sinceLast, method {baseRate 0.002, correlation 0.85, factorModel, globalModel, blackSwanDefinition}, factors[], drivers[], calendar[{d,t}], watch[{k,v,n}], countries[]. No "norway" object.
- Global factor: id, name, tag, severity 1–5, P, outcome, summary, sinceLast, trend ("up"|"down"|"flat"|"new"), drivers [driver ids], spill (use data/model.json globalSpill), sources [{t,u}], experts [].
- Driver: id, t, p, h (deadline or "by <date>"), why, imp {factorId: log-odds weight, typically 0.2–1.5, negative if it lowers risk}.
- countries[]: exactly the 8 objects defined in data/model.json, in this order: us, uk, fr, de, it, jp, ca, eu. Each has code, name, place, tab, baseRate, correlation, spill, sourcesNote (copy from model.json), plus factors[] (IDs, names, tags, severity and outcome texts copied from model.json and kept fixed; add P, summary, sinceLast, trend, advice (1–2 practical sentences for residents), sources, experts, drivers), drivers[] (3–6 national drivers, ids prefixed d_<code>_), links {globalDriverId: {nationalFactorId: weight}}, watch[] (3–6 key indicators), calendar[] (dated triggers in the next 30–60 days), headline, summary (4–6 sentences), sinceLast, method (one sentence: P(national crisis) = correlation × [1 − (1 − base rate) × Π(1 − P_factor × spill)], national crisis = an event requiring national or EU-level crisis management that affects large parts of the population for several days).

2. Research (WebSearch/WebFetch, focus on the last 24–72 hours, several searches per area): the 10 global factors; for each G7 country and the EU the six standard areas (government crisis/unrest, fiscal and bond-market stress, terrorism, state sabotage/cyber, energy supply, natural hazards) plus its specific factors (Italy water and migration, Japan North Korean missiles, Canada US trade, EU euro-area stress, cohesion, armed attack, energy, trade and migration). Check the levels behind each threshold (10-year yields and spreads, rating calendars, gas prices, threat levels).

Source standards:
- Favour knowledge over opinion: primary data and official sources (central banks, finance ministries, statistics offices, interior ministries and threat-level authorities, cyber agencies, grid operators, meteorological and geological services, IMF, OECD, ECB, European Commission, IAEA, WHO, FAO, NOAA/CPC, WMO, UKMTO), established research institutes, and regional specialists who live in or work from the country they cover. Use prediction markets/Metaculus where they exist.
- Balance across the political spectrum. For each contested topic use institutions from different traditions (e.g. AEI, Heritage, Hudson, FDD alongside Brookings, Carnegie, Crisis Group, Quincy; Chatham House, IISS, RUSI, RAND, CSIS, CEPA, ECFR, Bruegel) and national voices from each country, not only US or UK commentary. When serious experts disagree, say so briefly.
- Treat elections and parties neutrally: an election result is not itself a risk; model only measurable consequences (market stress, government collapse, unrest).
- Avoid partisan outlets, anonymous blogs, aggregators and content farms as primary evidence; cross-check key numbers against at least two sources. 3–6 sources per factor, each one you actually read today, with https URLs.

3. Probabilities (rolling 30 days): every P and p is the probability that the event happens within the next 30 days. Calibrate against base rates (how often such events happened in comparable 30-day periods over the last 10–20 years), then adjust for current conditions and dated catalysts inside the window. Change numbers only for a concrete reason, keep changes proportionate, and let probabilities roll as the window moves. Allowed range 0.003–0.99. Keep method.baseRate 0.002, national baseRate 0.001, correlation 0.85 and the spill maps from data/model.json unless there is a strong, stated reason. The Ukraine factor ("no general ceasefire in force at the end of the window") is a persistence indicator: global spill 0.005, national spill 0.002.

4. Text: headline (one sentence), globalSummary (4–6 sentences), sinceLast (what changed since the previous snapshot and which numbers moved), per factor summary and sinceLast ("No significant change" when true), per country headline, summary, sinceLast. English only. No quotation longer than 15 words from any one source.

5. Quality control: valid JSON; every driver id referenced in any factor's drivers list exists in drivers[] or a countries[].drivers[]; every key in links is an existing driver id and every key in spill an existing factor id; all URLs start with https://; the word "Flocean" appears nowhere.

6. Publish: write the snapshot to /tmp/snapshot.json and run "python3 tools/add_snapshot.py /tmp/snapshot.json" from the repo root (it writes data/snapshots/<date>.json and updates data/manifest.json). If today's file already exists, overwrite it. Do not change index.html unless it is broken. Commit with message "Snapshot YYYY-MM-DD" and push directly to main (not a claude/ branch). Wait about 2 minutes, then confirm with WebFetch that https://afuglesang.github.io/riskradar-g7/data/manifest.json lists today's date.

7. Notify: if the push or the site check fails, send a push notification saying the site was not updated and why, in one or two plain sentences. If the global shock probability moved more than 1.5 percentage points, a national crisis probability more than 2.5 points, or any factor more than 5 points, send a push notification with a 3–5 line summary of the largest changes. Otherwise end with a one-line confirmation.
